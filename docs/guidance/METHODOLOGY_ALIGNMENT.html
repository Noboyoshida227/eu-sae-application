Methodology alignment and QER repair notes

w5p update, October 28, 2026. The input/reporting repairs and synchronized guidance are described in the deviations and justification register and fresh validation receipt. The owner's subsequent revision changes the applicable variance default to sm_all, with matching MFH rho_sm_all, while retaining MFH2 and the joint-bootstrap definitions. Explicit saved policies and historical evidence retain their identity. Direct uncertainty reports raw survey variance separately from model working variance. T=2 supports the official-statistics revision policy explained below.

Date: 2026-10-10. w5n pre-release (QER candidate); see QER/VALIDATION_RECEIPT.md for validation evidence and the scope of the candidate build.

w5o (5.2.0-rc.6-wizard.5.15, 13 October 2026) keeps the estimation methods reviewed here. It changes the interface (now the EU SAE Workbench), corrects the population weights of survey files with one row per person (a new survey-unit setting; such files previously needed a household size of 1) and stops runs in which a domain belongs to more than one benchmark group. With household-level data its estimates are the same as w5n: in the Spain example check, all 117 output tables were identical. In w5o the 57-slide guide and its Polish and Greek versions are replaced by three short guides in English, Polish and Greek; see CHANGELOG.md.

The methodological references are Molina and Romero, Comparable Small Area Estimates over Time (the 29 January 2025 manuscript supplied by the maintainer), and Harmening and coauthors, A Framework for Producing Small Area Estimates Based on Area-Level Models in R (the supplied EMDI note). These references define the statistical principles. They do not specify every numerical, bootstrap or application decision. The implementation distinguishes those decisions below. Verification used R 4.5.2, emdi 2.2.3 and msae 0.1.5.

MFH3 Time-varying innovation variances; initial u0 ~ N(0,1), so u1=rho*u0+a1. Fitting and simulation use the same initial-state covariance. sae_mfh_random_effect_covariance, sae_simulate_mfh_random_effects

Selecting MFH3 starts the p.21 variance-structure sequence, falling back to MFH2 if its endpoint is unusable or the variance contrast does not support MFH3. R/mfh_model_selection.R, scripts/02_mfh.R, both app interfaces
Two-year publication window The manuscript supports general T. The application uses two successive survey years so an NSO can revise at most one previously published year per release, once for each year's estimate, then freeze it. This requires an operational policy of successive windows and frozen vintages; T=2 alone does not enforce it. Retain each pair's joint change and uncertainty separately from a series assembled from different frozen vintages. MOLINA_ROMERO_DEVIATIONS_w5p.md; both app interfaces
Default variance policy sm_all replaces all working variances with GVF predictions for identity/log UFH and MFH; the corresponding MFH covariance default is rho_sm_all. This follows the full-smoothing illustration on pp10–12, subject to GVF adequacy. Arcsine UFH retains effective-sample-size variance. Explicit sm_out/direct settings remain available and are not rewritten. scripts/01_ufh.R, scripts/02_mfh.R, both app interfaces
Change uncertainty M/R p.36: MSE(change)=MSE1+MSE2−2*MCPE. All three moments come from the same accepted bootstrap draws. Invalid joint moments yield unavailable inference, never an invented independence fallback. sae_mfh_bootstrap, sae_joint_error_moments
Multiplicity M/R p.37 accepts individual-area comparison, explains familywise correction such as Bonferroni, then cautions that many-area testing has poor power. Primary pointwise counts across areas are exploratory; BH and Bonferroni outputs address different error criteria. The separate MFH3 variance-contrast adjustment is discussed on p.23; there is only one contrast with two years. scripts/02_mfh.R, scripts/03_comparison.R
GVF retransformation Relative to M/R p.12's exponentiated fitted-log-variance illustration, use exp(predicted log variance + residual variance/2). This conditional-mean correction requires appropriate lognormal, homoscedastic residual assumptions; it is not universally unbiased. scripts/ufh_functions.R, R/variance_policy.R

MFH3's fixed initial variance of one also makes its covariance family sensitive to the measurement scale: for example, G11=innovation1+rho². This mathematical feature can contribute to difficult fitting on proportion-scale outcomes; it is not evidence that every MFH3 non-convergence is a software defect.

Explicit extensions and assumptions

The maintainer's October 28, 2026 clarification records the project rationale: the EMDI team recommended including benchmarking, and the Polish and Greek teams requested anchored poverty and mean welfare. Benchmarking is not discussed in the supplied Molina–Romero manuscript; the anchored-poverty and mean-welfare workflows are not explicitly specified there. These are application extensions. The requests explain their inclusion, not statistical validation or endorsement of every implementation detail. See the deviations note.

  • Anchored poverty: apply the FGT workflow to analyst-supplied poverty-line columns or year-specific numeric thresholds with a documented reference-year anchor and consistent purchasing power. Poverty runs do not use the mean-welfare CPI deflator. The app does not automatically construct a median-based anchor or update poverty lines for inflation; an unchanged nominal line is not generally a constant-real standard. Treat supplied lines and price indices as fixed inputs; uncertainty from estimating them is not automatically propagated.
  • Mean welfare: extend the indicator scope to survey-weighted arithmetic area means of the supplied welfare variable. Harmonize welfare definitions, income periods, row-unit weights, equivalence scales and price bases. Optional price conversion precedes aggregation; distinguish nominal and real changes. Optional log fitting targets the arithmetic mean and requires positive finite area means, with the transformation and uncertainty qualifications below.
  • Bootstrap: the supplied M/R manuscript does not develop a complete MCPE bootstrap. The package's extension generates correlated random effects and sampling errors, refits the same model and accumulates prediction-error products. MSE is a second moment, not a centered sample covariance. A draw is accepted jointly only when the fit and all predictions are usable.
  • MFH log prediction: the paper's linear MFH formulas do not prescribe a bias-corrected currency estimator. The package uses the fitted Gaussian conditional mean exp(eta + v/2), with v=diag(G-G(G+R)^(-1)G). Annual MSE and MCPE are bootstrapped after inverse transformation. This is not emdi's univariate Slud-Maiti correction. Saved MFH settings named bc_sm are accepted as a legacy alias for this new method.
  • Benchmarking: offer consistency with selected national/group aggregates, following the EMDI team's recommendation to include this option. Compatible target populations, periods and units are required; exact reconciliation is not evidence of better accuracy or coverage. Perform the final ratio adjustment in the reported units inside every bootstrap replicate. Recompute survey-derived targets from that replicate's direct estimates. Keep uploaded external targets fixed; their uncertainty is not modeled. Conditional bootstrap calculations hold auxiliary variables, chosen formulas, the selected MFH type, populations and sampling covariance estimates fixed. They do not repeat variable selection, MFH3/MFH2 selection, GVF fitting or covariance estimation in each draw, so those sources of uncertainty are excluded.
  • Numerical fitting: a package convergence flag alone is insufficient. Check REML score, local curvature and feasible boundary directions. Refit MFH2 if needed, retain a valid original fit, and reject an inferior refit. True zero-variance endpoints remain eligible for bootstrap inference. The stationary AR(1) correlation is numerically bounded at ±0.999999; an accepted endpoint there approximates a limiting fit, not an interior maximum. Selected annual uncertainty uses bootstrap on both scales.
  • Sampling covariance: repair invalid domain sampling covariance matrices once before original fitting, then use exactly those matrices for every bootstrap simulation and refit. Record changes in mfh_sampling_covariance.csv. Sampling-correlation smoothing and the optional application-specific sm_out threshold of 0.001 remain disclosed choices, not universal rules supplied by either reference. For log means the threshold applies to the log-scale variance. The new sm_all default does not use this cutoff; it remains conditional on the fitted GVF and does not propagate GVF-estimation uncertainty.
  • UFH changes: separate yearly UFH fits retain the explicitly exported period_independence assumption. A joint survey covariance model has not been invented for these fits. This limitation matters with overlapping samples or longitudinal dependence.
  • Mean percentage changes: use a first-order log-ratio variance based on the same joint original-scale moments, and exponentiate the interval. Annual and change uncertainty must never be mixed across estimators.
  • Diagnostics: MFH residual plots use (Direct−EBLUP)/sqrt(diag(RPR)), where P accounts for fitted fixed effects. Residuals remain correlated; Shapiro-Wilk and residual-versus-fitted regressions are descriptive checks, not exact independent-sample tests.

EMDI discrepancies that must remain visible

The supplied note p.5 displays a full-real-line integral for corrected arcsine prediction. The tested emdi 2.2.3 integrates over [0, pi/2]. The package retains that point-estimator behavior for compatibility and bootstraps that same estimator. It reports substantial Gaussian mass above pi/2 (over 1%) because upper-boundary truncation can have a large effect. This is an upstream paper-versus-package discrepancy, not evidence that the correction is unbiased at every boundary. The naive bootstrap now uses the same clamp as its point estimator, unlike the unclamped upstream bootstrap expression.

Native emdi bc_sm does not supply corrected log predictions for unsampled areas. Those values remain unavailable; benchmarking requires complete usable predictions for the requested benchmark population. No ad hoc smearing factor is substituted.

Evidence and implications for circulation

The added tests compare UFH predictions and MSE directly with installed emdi; check MFH bootstrap identities independently; compare block REML algebra with dense calculations; exercise zero/correlation boundaries and failed fits; and verify final currency-scale benchmark constraints. The oracle simulation uses independent Gaussian/lognormal formulas. Fitted simulation pilots are separate from those exact-parameter checks. See MCPE_VALIDATION_STATUS.md for the evidence and its limits. The larger MFH2 study uses 2,100 outer samples and 500–1,000 bootstrap draws. Ordinary designs have approximately 94.3–94.9% absolute-change coverage against nominal 95%. A low-signal design has 87.4%; its 110 correlation-limit fits have 65.2%. These are scenario-specific results, not universal rates. Boundary-sensitive inference is now explicitly flagged as provisional. Increasing bootstrap draws alone cannot repair this limitation.

Revising documentation alone would not address these defects. Log-welfare point estimates, uncertainty, change significance and some MFH2 poverty estimates can change. On the saved Spanish fitting frame, the checked MFH2 restricted log-likelihood improves from 170.397 to 172.771 and the largest annual point-estimate change is about 8.05 percentage points. This is a specific saved-run comparison, not a forecast for every country or setup. The fitted correlation reaches the numerical stationary limit; inspect its bootstrap diagnostics and sensitivity before using the results externally.

The w5n QER candidate includes revised English guidelines, four technical notes, the 57-slide guide, download instructions, app help and report prose. Historical figures in the guidance are explicitly illustrative. New isolated Spanish example runs exercise the revised calculations; archived country setups still require review and rerunning before their results are circulated.
